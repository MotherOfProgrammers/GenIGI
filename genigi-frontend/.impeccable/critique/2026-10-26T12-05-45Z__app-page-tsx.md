## Design Specificity Verdict

**The words are authored. The interface is not.**

**LLM assessment.** The copy is unmistakably GenIGI: “The mission isn’t over,” “No made-up release dates,” “For the people who never really left the field,” “The quiet before a patrol turns.” The field-notes vocabulary, the radar motif and the refusal to fake downloads are a real point of view. But the visual grammar underneath is three stacked genre skins: red/black neon gaming, navy/cyan/purple sci-fi HUD, and a white/pink light theme that turns the memorial into a festival poster. The site about a 2000 tactical shooter never shows the game — only an abstract radar and contour. The “Field notes 01” label is decoration, not a real dated log. The result could be a mod page for almost any military-adjacent product.

**Deterministic scan.** CLI: **7 findings, all in `app/globals.css`** — 6 warnings, 1 advisory (exit 2). Rules: `side-tab` ×3 (`:148`, `:503`, `:570`), `gradient-text` ×3 (`:397`, `:555`, `:703`), `codex-grid-background` advisory (`:379`). The browser overlay (headless injection on `/` and `/mods`, dark and light) added viewport-dependent evidence the static scan cannot see: **29 anti-patterns on `/` dark, 28 light; 13 on `/mods`** — led by `dark-glow` (18/17), `undersized-ui-text` (2 on home, 5 on mods), `low-contrast` (2/1), plus `gradient-text`, `kicker-above-heading`, `numbered-section-labels`, `straggler`. Most consequential overlay-only catches: **white text on the white stop of the gradient badge at 1.0:1**, **`a.button` at 1.1–1.2:1** where the work-band CTA crosses its white gradient stop, and **8–10px interface text**. ## Overall Impression

The first screen is the best thing here: specific, atmospheric, unafraid of space. After that, the site becomes a handsome but generic gaming shell with three identities and no working exits. The single biggest opportunity is not “more polish” — it is to make the honest field-notes idea the interface: real dated logs, mission-dossier cards, actual people, and one working way in. Right now the words promise a thoughtful preservation community; the structure delivers a brochure with dead buttons.

## What’s Working

1. **A real editorial voice.** The copy is restrained, specific and emotionally legible. “Make the way back feel welcoming. Keep the mission itself intact.” is a brand line, not filler. Do not flatten it into “next-generation tactical experience.”
2. **The hero composition and display type.** The asymmetrical poster, Tourney’s armored letterforms, the red “OVER.” punchline and the radar create a strong first impression and reflow intelligently to 820px and 390px.
3. **Honesty as a brand asset.** “Good ideas. No made-up release dates.” and the mods status banner are genuine differentiators. Systematize that — don’t contradict it with a “COMING SOON” feature drop and a live-looking auth form.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The auth forms are dead, but dressed as live
**What:** `/login` and `/register` render complete forms; both use `onSubmit={(e) => e.preventDefault()}`. Submitting leaves the URL, body text and focus unchanged — no success, error, alert or disabled state. The homepage says accounts are “planned, but not live yet”; the auth pages never mention it, even though an unused `.account-alert` style already exists.
**Why it matters:** The highest-intent action on the site silently fails. Silence reads as broken or dishonest, and it directly contradicts the “no imaginary release buttons” ethos.
**Fix:** Pick one honest state: wire real auth, or show an unmissable “not live yet” panel, disable submit with explanation, remove the “Register here” invitations, and offer a working Discord/update-signup alternative. Add autocomplete, password visibility, confirm-match validation, requirements and an `aria-live` result region.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] Three visual identities — and concrete reading failures
**What:** Red/black neon in the header, ticker, work bands and Rampage; navy/cyan/purple in the hero default, quote card, auth panels, 404 and footer; white/pink throughout light theme. Leftover cyan pills, lime status dots and multicolor emoji remain. `globals.css` has two identical `:root` blocks, a second “gaming theme” layer and a ~250-line light override.
**Why it matters:** It feels assembled, and it breaks reading:
- `/about` dark: quote is **black on a #0a1420 card — ~1.13:1**. The quote is effectively invisible while its caption remains readable, so it looks like a rendering bug.
- Light theme: “IGI RAMPAGE” uses a gradient containing `#ffffff` on a white card — **1:1 at the white stop**. “COMING SOON” white-on-white is **1.0:1**; the work-band CTA is **1.1–1.2:1**. The 404 secondary button and pale “OVER.” also wash out.
- Red means brand, primary action, warning, error, hover and focus simultaneously. Nothing can be urgent.
**Fix:** Choose one system and delete the rest. The navy/cyan skin is the unowned one. Build semantic tokens (surface, text, muted, accent, success, warning, danger) and use them in both themes. Ban gradient-clipped text on arbitrary surfaces; give every gradient a solid fallback and audit both themes at 4.5:1 body / 3:1 large text.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable colorize`

### [P1] Every path into the work or the community dead-ends
**What:** `/mods` has no mods, downloads, detail pages or submit path; its three non-clickable rows animate on hover, implying they open. Four CTAs (“Explore the work,” “See community directions,” “Browse the work,” “Explore directions”) all land there. The Discord icon — the only community channel — points to `https://discord.com`, not a server. `/about` has no people, team, player quotes or contribution path; the only human trace is four 10px footer names. “Account status →” leads to the dead register form.
**Why it matters:** A community site whose every conversion path fails cannot build trust, however good the hero is. The honesty copy makes the false labels (“Mods,” “Meet the community,” “Account status”) more damaging.
**Fix:** Rename the route to “Workboard”/“Roadmap” until it earns “Mods.” Make each direction a dossier with owner, stage, last update and a Contribute action. Add a real Discord invite and a visible text CTA; add people and roles to `/about`; remove hover movement from non-interactive rows; give “Account status” a real status destination.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] The first screen fails on common laptop heights, and the mobile header is squeezed
**What:** Hero is **808.5px** under a **79px** header = **887.5px**. At 1366×768 the CTA starts at y=759.5, leaving **8.5px** visible; at 1280×720 it is off-screen. On mobile, the header carries 8 controls: the five text links are **14px tall**, Register 34px, Discord 30×30, Mode 58×30 — all below 44×44. At 360px the nav is **345px inside a 324px scroller** and “MODE” clips to “MO.”
**Why it matters:** The primary action is invisible on common laptops, and mobile users get a cramped strip where Login, Register, Discord and theme are easy to mis-tap. The header consumes 13% of an 844px screen before content.
**Fix:** Clamp the hero to `calc(100svh - header)` with a sensible min; reduce type/padding under `@media (max-height: 800px)` and verify CTA visibility at 1280×720 and 1366×768. Replace the wrapping nav with a 44px-target menu/sheet on small screens, move theme and Discord inside it, and mark the active page with `aria-current`.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] The Rampage block contradicts the site’s own promise
**What:** Seven emoji bullets in identical red bars, a gradient-border “COMING SOON” card and “Redefining tactical stealth for a new generation.” It is the largest section on the homepage and the most category-interchangeable.
**Why it matters:** The previous section promises no hype; this is AAA launch grammar with no screenshot, date or proof. It is also the visual opposite of the site’s restrained tactical language.
**Fix:** Cut to 3–4 real, dated items with status and proof; replace emoji with monochrome icons or numbered markers; remove the badge and gradient wordmark or make them solid. Let a dated devlog carry the section.
**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

## Minor Observations

- Ticker starts mid-word (“HE MISSION INTACT”) and is `aria-hidden`, so its message reaches no screen reader.
- CSS styles `[aria-current="page"]` but the markup never sets it; every page’s nav looks identical.
- “MODE” is static in both themes, with a sun icon either way and no `aria-pressed`.
- 404’s “ERR_CODE: 0x404,” `[SYS]/[SEC]/[CAL]` lines and “Error has been logged” are invented system theater that breaks the straight-with-you brand.
- All three `/mods` chips use the same green dot for “early,” “in progress” and “community principle”; “principle” is not a status.
- Page titles for `/login`, `/register` and 404 all reuse the default title.
- Type range is 17 steps (138→8px); Tourney is used at 25–27px where it reads worse than body; Barlow Condensed is imported but never rendered.
- “North star” is the site quoting itself in testimonial styling — inviting a search for a person who isn’t there.
- Footer “Project IGI source on GitHub” is ambiguous (community project vs original game).
- Light theme is a 250-line patch over the dark system; every new component will need one more override.

## Questions to Consider

1. What would the first screen look like if one real IGI artifact — a mission map, a before/after compatibility shot, a dated field note — carried it instead of a radar that could belong to any military-adjacent product?
2. Why is “Register” the loudest, most repeated CTA when the site says accounts are not live? What if “Join the Discord” were the only header CTA until registration ships?
3. If “no made-up release dates” is the promise, why does Rampage use launch-day grammar? Would a dated, unglamorous devlog be more persuasive than a feature drop with no screenshots?
4. What if `/mods` were a mission board — each direction a dossier with owner, stage, last update, Contribute — so the absence of downloads read as visible progress?
5. Does this need two themes, or is the toggle hiding an unresolved brand decision? Which one is GenIGI: tactical red/black, warm poster, or navy HUD? Today the answer is all three.
